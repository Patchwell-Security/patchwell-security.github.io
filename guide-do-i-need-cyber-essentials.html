Do I need Cyber Essentials?

When you have to have it, when it's simply worth having, what it costs, and the government discount that cuts the fee by 75% until March 2027. About 8 minutes to read.

Based on the government's Procurement Policy Note 014 (February 2025), the Ministry of Defence's supplier guidance, and the Cyber Essentials funding announced on 3 November 2026. Checked on 23 November 2026.

The short answer

No law says every business must have Cyber Essentials. You need it when a customer or contract asks for it. That most often means:

  • bidding for certain central government or NHS contracts
  • supplying the Ministry of Defence, or a company that does
  • a larger customer asking all its suppliers to be certified

Even if nobody has asked yet, it's often worth getting. It comes with free cyber insurance for most small businesses, and right now the fee is 75% cheaper than usual.

Government and NHS contracts

Since 2014, the government has required suppliers bidding for certain types of public contract to hold Cyber Essentials or Cyber Essentials Plus, or to show they have equivalent controls. The current rules are in Procurement Policy Note (PPN) 014, which covers procurements that started on or after 24 February 2025.

  • Who it applies to: central government departments, their agencies and public bodies, and NHS bodies.
  • Which contracts: not all of them. Buyers are told not to apply it as a blanket rule. It's mainly for contracts where you would handle personal information or provide certain IT systems and services.
  • When you need it: usually before the contract is awarded, not after you win.

Councils aren't named in PPN 014, but many ask for Cyber Essentials in their own tenders. The tender documents will say. Look for "Cyber Essentials" in the requirements or the supplier questionnaire.

Ministry of Defence contracts

The MOD uses its own Cyber Security Model. Each contract gets a cyber risk rating:

  • Very Low risk: you need Cyber Essentials.
  • Low risk or higher: you need Cyber Essentials Plus, which adds a hands-on technical test.

These requirements can be passed down the supply chain, so a small subcontractor to a defence company may be asked too.

Larger customers and supply chains

Big companies increasingly ask suppliers for Cyber Essentials, because attackers often get in through a smaller supplier. Over 230 organisations have signed the government's Cyber Resilience Pledge, which commits them to a risk-based approach to asking for Cyber Essentials in their supply chains. If a customer emails asking whether you're certified, this is usually why.

Worth having even if nobody's asked

  • Free cyber insurance: organisations with Cyber Essentials and turnover under £20m get up to £25,000 of cyber liability insurance (terms apply, and the whole organisation has to be certified).
  • Fewer successful attacks: the five controls block the most common attacks, such as stolen passwords and known holes in old software.
  • Trust: you can show the certificate and logo to customers. It lasts 12 months and is renewed each year.

What it costs

IASME, which runs the scheme for the NCSC, sets one price for the basic Cyber Essentials assessment based on how many employees you have:

Micro (0–9 employees)£320 + VAT£80 + VAT
Small (10–49)£440 + VAT£110 + VAT
Medium (50–249)£500 + VAT£125 + VAT

Some certification bodies add their own charges for extra help on top of the IASME fee. Cyber Essentials Plus is priced separately by each certification body and costs more.

The 75% discount: Cyber Essentials First Step Fund
  • Who can get it: UK organisations with fewer than 250 employees that are certifying for the first time, or haven't held Cyber Essentials in the last four years.
  • Deadline: you must buy the assessment before 31 March 2027, and the money is first come, first served, so it may run out sooner.
  • Time to finish: after you buy, you have six months to complete and submit your assessment.
  • How to apply: check your eligibility at firststep.iasme.co.uk, or ask any Cyber Essentials certification body to check for you.
A separate grant pays the whole fee for some suppliers of companies that signed the Cyber Resilience Pledge. You can't apply for that one yourself; the company you supply has to nominate you.

So, do you need it? A quick check

  • A tender, contract or customer has asked for it → yes, and soon. Check whether they want Cyber Essentials or Cyber Essentials Plus.
  • You plan to bid for public-sector or defence work in the next year → yes. Get it before you bid, as it's usually needed before the contract is awarded.
  • You hold customer data or take payments online → worth it, especially with the insurance and the discount.
  • You work alone, hold no customer data and nobody has asked → optional. You'll still benefit from following the five controls.

The discount lowers the fee, not the bar

The assessment questions are the same at any price. Since April 2026, two things fail you automatically: missing two-step sign-in (MFA) on any cloud service that offers it, and high-risk updates not installed within 14 days.
